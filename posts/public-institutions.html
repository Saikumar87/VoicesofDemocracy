The Architecture of the Republic: Parliament, Courts and India's Constitutional Institutions

How India's Constitution distributes power and protects democratic government.

India's Constitution is more than a legal document. It establishes the institutions of the Republic, defines their powers and sets limits on how public authority may be exercised.

The Constitution and its values

The Constitution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950. Its Preamble describes India as a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic and commits the state to justice, liberty, equality and fraternity.

Parliament

At the Union level, Parliament consists of the President and two Houses: the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha. The Lok Sabha is directly elected by citizens. The Rajya Sabha represents the states and Union territories through its constitutional election arrangements and nominated members.

Parliament makes laws, debates public policy, approves government expenditure and holds the Union executive accountable through questions, discussions and committees.

Federalism and the states

India has a federal constitutional structure with powers distributed between the Union and state governments. The Seventh Schedule sets out the Union List, State List and Concurrent List, identifying areas where different levels of government may legislate.

Federalism allows national coordination while recognising the importance of state-level government in a country of immense regional diversity.

The judiciary

The Supreme Court of India is the highest court in the country's judicial system. High Courts and subordinate courts operate across the states and territories. Courts interpret the Constitution, resolve legal disputes and review whether government action is consistent with constitutional law.

The basic structure doctrine

In the 1973 Kesavananda Bharati judgment, the Supreme Court held that Parliament's power to amend the Constitution does not extend to destroying its basic structure. The doctrine has become an important feature of Indian constitutional law, although its application depends on judicial interpretation in individual cases.

Independent constitutional institutions

The Constitution also establishes or provides for institutions intended to perform specific public functions, including the Election Commission, the Comptroller and Auditor General, and the Union Public Service Commission. Their roles differ, but each contributes to the constitutional framework of public administration and accountability.

Why institutional independence matters

Democratic government requires more than elected leaders. It also depends on rules, oversight and institutions capable of carrying out their responsibilities lawfully and impartially.
