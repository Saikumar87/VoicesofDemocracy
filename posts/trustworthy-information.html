Democracy Needs Reliable Information: Press Freedom and the Lessons of India's Emergency

Why access to information and independent reporting matter to citizens.

Citizens cannot meaningfully evaluate government decisions if they cannot obtain reliable information about public affairs. A free press, public records and open debate help people understand what governments do and hold public institutions accountable.

Freedom of speech and expression

Article 19(1)(a) of the Indian Constitution guarantees citizens freedom of speech and expression. Article 19(2) allows reasonable restrictions on specified grounds, including the sovereignty and integrity of India, security of the state, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation and incitement to an offence.

Freedom of the press is not separately named as an independent right in Article 19, but the Supreme Court has recognised press freedom as part of the freedom of speech and expression.

Press censorship during the Emergency

During the national Emergency from 1975 to 1977, press censorship and restrictions on civil liberties sharply reduced the space for public criticism. The period is a significant chapter in India's democratic history because it shows how quickly access to information and public debate can be constrained.

The Right to Information Act, 2005

The RTI Act created a statutory framework through which citizens can request information from public authorities. It is an important tool for transparency, although the Act includes exemptions and procedural requirements.

Access to official documents can help citizens, journalists and researchers examine public spending, administrative decisions and the implementation of government programmes.

How to assess information

  • Check whether a claim is supported by primary documents or reliable reporting.
  • Look for the date, author and original context of a statement.
  • Compare coverage from more than one credible source.
  • Distinguish reporting, opinion, satire and political advertising.
  • Be cautious with images, videos and statistics shared without context.

Why this matters

Reliable information helps voters evaluate competing claims, understand public policy and identify errors or abuses. It does not require citizens to agree with one another. It gives them a stronger factual basis for disagreement.
